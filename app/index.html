    Method

    Reading a hotel stripe

    Like climate warming stripes (one stripe per year) or a film color barcode (one sliver per frame), a Colorways stripe compresses a whole stay into one band. It reads left to right as a stay: arrival, lobby, room, bath, morning, pool, spa, dinner, drinks, night out.

    • Each block is a key space. Blocks are separated by a thin gap.
    • Block width follows the weighting you choose. Area uses square feet (published, measured, or a labeled default for that space type). Time uses typical hours a guest spends there. Equal gives every space the same width.
    • Inside a block, each color gets width in proportion to its share of that space.
    • The key swatch (five chips) is the simplified read: the most striking colors, weighted toward photo-op regions, vivid colors, and spaces with high photo-op scores.

    Why three weightings

    Square footage measures the building, not the experience. A 60,000 sq ft casino would drown a 450 sq ft guest room, even though guests spend most of their stay in the room. Time weighting corrects for that; equal weighting is the cleanest comparison between hotels.

    Where colors come from

    Color families

    Every hex is converted to CIELAB (a perceptual color space) and sorted into one of 15 families by lightness (L*), chroma (C*, how colorful) and hue angle (h). The same rules run in this app and in the Excel workbook.

    Space types and default assumptions

    Defaults are planning assumptions used only when a space has no published or measured area or dwell time. They are labeled "Type default" everywhere.

    Limits

    • Photos measure what the camera saw: framing, white balance, and time of day all shift color. Pixel share is visual share as photographed, not literal surface area.
    • Described colors are estimates of real, sourced colors. They are placeholders until measured.
    • Search uses CIEDE2000, the current standard for perceived color difference; under about 2 most people see no difference, above about 10 colors read as different.
